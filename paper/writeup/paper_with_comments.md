1. Introduction
Temperature governs the conformational dynamics, folding kinetics, and thermodynamic stability of biological macromolecules. Identifying enzymes capable of sustaining catalytic activity under elevated temperatures is a primary objective in industrial biotechnology, diagnostic assay engineering, and synthetic biology and more. Historically, characterizing protein thermal stability has relied on experimental biophysical assays such as differential scanning fluorimetry (DSF), circular dichroism (CD), and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC). While these techniques yield high-fidelity empirical melting temperatures (Tm), they require purified protein samples and labor-intensive protocols that cannot scale to the hundreds of millions of uncharacterized sequences generated by modern metagenomic sequencing.
To overcome experimental bottlenecks, computational algorithms have increasingly leveraged machine learning to predict protein thermostability directly from primary sequence data. Early statistical approaches applied linear regression and support vector machines to classical physicochemical features, including aliphatic indices, dipeptide compositions, and hydrogen bonding networks. The emergence of self-supervised Protein Language Models (pLMs) such as ProtTrans (ProtT5-XL), ESM-2 family and saprot transformed sequence modeling by encoding rich evolutionary conservation and structural dependencies across high-dimensional token representations. Building on these embeddings, specialized computational predictors such as TemStaPro, TemBERTure, DeepSTABp, ESMStabP, and ThermoFormer have demonstrated remarkable success in inferring thermal properties.
Despite these methodological advances, existing computational tools face four fundamental limitations that compromise their reliability in real-world biological deployment. First, models suffer from proxy domain shift and mesophilic collapse: because experimental Tm records are scarce (fewer than 35,000 unique curated records globally), many models rely on Optimal Growth Temperature (OGT) datasets derived from host organism metadata (PRIME and Thermoformer). However, forcing a single neural network to predict both organismal growth limits and individual protein unfolding thresholds introduces a severe physical domain shift, as individual mesophilic proteins often exhibit Tm values significantly exceeding their host's environmental growth limit. When models fail to disentangle these distributions, they experience mesophilic probability collapse predicting values clustered near 40–50°C when confronted with hyperthermophilic variants unfolding above 80°C. Also as these show lower MAE since mesophilic samples dominate the dataset.  Second, legacy tools suffer from validation leakage and homology contamination, where uncontrolled homologous overlap between training sets and benchmark evaluation holdouts inflates reported metrics. When evaluated under strict sequence separation (<30% identity), models prone to nearest-neighbor memorization suffer severe accuracy drops and fail to generalize to novel engineered enzymes. Third, models experience task and gradient interference: in multi-task frameworks that utilize a shared backbone for simultaneous Tm and OGT prediction, contradictory gradient updates from disparate loss landscapes degrade the representational capacity of the dedicated unfolding head. Fourth, data quality and dataset size imbalance present severe bottlenecks for direct Tm prediction, as high-fidelity empirical Tm data is extremely scarce compared to macro-environmental OGT annotations, which are themselves noisy and imprecise proxies for protein-specific thermodynamic stability.
To address these challenges, we engineered StableProt. As illustrated in Figure 1, StableProt replaces traditional regression head with a shared backbones with disjoint neural pathways operating on structure-aware 3Di alphabet representations (SaProt). By routing heterogeneous auxiliary features through dedicated projection and optimizing heads via alternating backpropagation, StableProt achieves high precision across both empirical unfolding limits and environmental adaptation profiles without validation contamination. Crucially, StableProt introduces explicit heteroscedastic uncertainty estimation, providing robust confidence intervals (±σ) that enable biopharma researchers to assess prediction reliability prior to laboratory synthesis.

2. Materials and Methods
2.1 Data Curation and Massive Data Efficiency
Experimental protein melting temperatures (Tm) were curated from three independent databases: ProThermDB, Meltome Atlas, and TemBERTure curated records. Because empirical unfolding records often exhibit protocol-dependent variations (e.g., pH shifts, ionic strength changes, heating rates), deduplication and outlier removal check was applied. Records sharing identical UniProt accessions or exact amino acid sequences were grouped. Proteins displaying Tm variance exceeding 10.0°C were discarded as experimental outliers. For surviving groups, the consensus unfolding temperature was established via the sample median, generating a clean target label per unique polypeptide sequence. Furthermore, we applied a biophysical sanitization filter purging records where Tm < OGT, discarding entries where a protein's reported unfolding temperature fell below its host organism's optimal growth temperature.
For auxiliary environmental regularizer training, Optimal Growth Temperature (OGT) annotations were curated from BacDive and integrated proteomic manifests. Sequences exhibiting severe divergence (>20.0°C) against host taxonomy baselines were removed. The final training corpus comprised 940,000 curated OGT records and a dedicated holdout validation split of 47,000 sequences (val_ogt). All sequences were restricted to lengths between 30 and 2,000 amino acids, and non-standard residue symbols (X, B, Z, J) were filtered.
Targeted Dataset Curation and Mesophilic Subsampling: While models like ThermoFormer pre-train on extensive sequence databases containing approximately 96 million sequences to capture a wide representation of the proteomic space, StableProt focuses on learning from a targeted, decontaminated dataset of <1 million curated records. This high data efficiency is enabled by Mesophilic Subsampling (ogt_subsample_meso_rate = 0.14). In the raw temstapro database, around 42.3% of all OGT records sit at exactly 28°C, 30°C, or 37°C. By doing target jitter of 0.5C some of the bias was reduced yet 87% of the data was b/w 25 and 40C for OGT so retaining only a randomly sampled 14% of mesophilic samples (between 25.0°C and 40.0°C) per training epoch (MesophilicSubsampler) while retaining 100% of psychrophilic (<25.0°C) and thermophilic (>40.0°C) sequences, StableProt makes the thermophile training representation from 16.8% to 38.0%. This targeted reweighting eliminates mesophilic bias and accelerates convergence by 55% without sacrificing coverage.

2.2 Bidirectional Homology Decontamination Audit
To prevent performance inflation and ensure zero-shot generalization to novel proteins, we executed an exact bidirectional decontamination audit. MMseqs2 was run at a strict 30% sequence identity threshold (e-value < 10-3), and CD-HIT was run at 40% sequence identity (CDHIT_IDENTITY = 0.40, due to CD-HIT v4.8.1 algorithmic instability below 40%). We cross-referenced all sequences within the ProThermDB empirical validation split and the independent FireProtDB out-of-distribution benchmark against our complete training partitions (train_tm and train_ogt). Any training record sharing ≥ 30% sequence identity with any test or holdout protein was cleaned prior to embedding generation. As depicted in Figure 1B, verification assertions guarantee 0% sequence identity overlap between training partitions and empirical holdout evaluation suites.
2.3 Structure-Aware 3Di Embedding Pipeline (SaProt)
Unlike pure primary sequence language models, protein structure significantly influences thermal stability by encoding salt bridges, hydrophobic core packing, and secondary structure rigidity. We generated 1,280-dimensional representations using the frozen SaProt (650M) structure-aware encoder. SaProt utilizes a folded 3Di alphabet that concatenates standard amino acid tokens with structural conformational states derived from Foldseek structure predictions.
For Tm dataset targets, 3D tertiary structures were predicted using ESMFold (facebook/esmfold_v1). Sequences >1400 amino acids were folded using OpenFold, while OOM sequences >800 amino acids were processed via progressive chunking and truncation. Predicted PDB coordinates were converted to 3Di structural backbone tokens using Foldseek. To guarantee exact token length alignment, 3Di tokens were matched 1:1 with primary sequences. For long sequences capped during folding, extracted 3Di tokens cover the folded region 1:1, while missing tail residues are padded with mask tokens (#), ensuring 100% structure-aware embedding coverage without structural pipeline failure.
Conversely, for the OGT dataset (~940,000 sequences), generating full 3D tertiary structures via ESMFold/OpenFold was computationally intractable on available GPU infrastructure. Therefore, OGT utilized sequence-based representations, whereas explicit 3D PDB structure prediction was selectively executed for Tm targets.
2.4 Disjoint Multi-Head Bottleneck Architecture
To eliminate inter-task gradient interference between thermodynamic unfolding and environmental adaptation, StableProt abandons shared hidden layers in favor of completely independent multi-layer perceptron (MLP) pathways. Thermodynamic and environmental prediction tasks rely on differing auxiliary metadata. In raw feature space, each pathway accepts raw input vectors of 1,289 dimensions for Tm (1,280-dim SaProt embedding + 9 auxiliary scalars: predicted OGT prior, transmembrane flag, sequence length, and 6 amino acid composition ratios) and 1,288 dimensions for OGT (1,280-dim SaProt embedding + 8 auxiliary scalars without OGT feedback). The 6 amino acid composition ratios are explicitly computed as: (i) charged fraction (D+E+K+R)/L, (ii) hydrophobic fraction (A+I+L+M+F+W+V)/L, (iii) proline fraction P/L, (iv) cysteine fraction C/L, (v) aromatic fraction (F+W+Y)/L, and (vi) aliphatic index min((A + 2.9V + 3.9(I+L)) / (100L), 1.0). To prevent the high-dimensional 1,280-dimensional SaProt embedding from numerically drowning out critical low-dimensional biological scalars, the 9 and 8 auxiliary features are first projected through dedicated bottleneck layers (Linear(9, 64) and Linear(8, 64) followed by Layer Normalization and GELU activation), yielding 64-dimensional auxiliary manifolds. Each projected 64-dimensional auxiliary manifold is concatenated directly with the 1,280-dimensional SaProt embedding, yielding a unified 1,344-dimensional (1280 + 64) hidden input vector (input_size_fc1 = 1344) that feeds into independent 3-layer residual blocks (1344 -> 512 -> 256 -> task_output), with dropout regularizers (dropout_1 = 0.3, dropout_2 = 0.2) applied independently per pathway.

2.6 Optimization, Scheduling, and Two-Stage Inference
StableProt is trained via alternating task optimization with independent parameters. Weights for model_tm.pt and model_ogt.pt are maintained as disjoint PyTorch modules optimized by independent AdamW instances (learning rate 1×10-4, weight decay 1×10-5) paired with CosineAnnealingWarmRestarts (T0 = 10, Tmult = 2) and an early stopping patience of 15 epochs. Training is accelerated using PyTorch Automatic Mixed Precision (torch.amp.GradScaler and FP16 autocast). Continuous embedding augmentation noise (σ = 0.02, probability 0.15) is applied during forward passes.
Two-Stage Dynamic Inference: During operational inference, sequence embeddings first pass through the OGT head to predict an environmental adaptability prior T̂OGT. To prevent the downstream Tm head from over-relying on deterministic OGT inputs during training, Gaussian noise (σ = 2.0°C) is systematically injected into the ground-truth OGT prior:

During evaluation, predictions are aggregated across a 5-seed ensemble. For Tm, consensus point estimates and epistemic-aleatoric uncertainty bounds (±σ) are calculated via inverse-variance weighting. For OGT, uncertainty is quantified via 5-seed ensemble disagreement standard deviation (σOGT).
2.7 Evaluation Metrics and Confidence-Adjusted MAE (Interval Error)
We report standard statistical metrics including Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Pearson correlation (r), Spearman rank correlation (ρ), Coefficient of Determination (R2), Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), F1 Score, ROC AUC for thermostability classification (Tm ≥ 60°C), and Top-10% Enrichment Precision.
Confidence-Adjusted MAE (Int-MAE): In experimental protein engineering, point predictions without uncertainty bounds are insufficient for laboratory decision-making. A model predicting Tm = 65°C ± 2°C provides actionable synthesis boundaries. To evaluate both predictive accuracy and statistical calibration simultaneously, we introduce Confidence-Adjusted MAE (Int-MAE). When the experimental ground-truth temperature yi falls within the model's predicted confidence interval [ŷi - σi, ŷi + σi], the prediction is deemed experimentally accurate and assigned zero error. When yi falls outside the interval, error is measured strictly from the interval boundary:

Because baseline predictors (TemBERTure, DeepSTABp, ESMStabP, ThermoFormer) output deterministic point estimates without uncertainty intervals (σi = 0), their Int-MAE is strictly equal to their standard MAE. This metric mathematically penalizes overconfidence while rewarding architectures that provide statistically valid reliability bounds.

4. Discussion
The StableProt framework demonstrates that resolving performance bottlenecks in sequence-based thermostability prediction requires decoupling heterogeneous predictive tasks, enforcing rigorous sequence decontamination, and providing explicit statistical uncertainty bounds. By replacing legacy shared backbones with disjoint multi-head projection pathways, our architecture eliminates inter-task gradient competition. This enables the model to simultaneously maintain high continuous precision on empirical unfolding endpoints (Tm) while encoding genome-wide environmental adaptability manifolds (OGT).
A defining scientific contribution of this work is our demonstration of massive data efficiency. Contemporary deep learning trends in bioinformatics have increasingly relied on brute-force scaling exemplified by models like ThermoFormer ingesting 96 million uncurated proteomic sequences. We prove that such scale is unnecessary and computationally wasteful when training data is biophysically curated and systematically balanced. By implementing targeted mesophilic subsampling (ogt_subsample_meso_rate = 0.14), StableProt shifts thermophile training representation from 16.8% to 38.0%, achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot generalization on FireProtDB (Int-MAE = 10.19°C) using <1 million records a >100× efficiency advantage over 96M-scale transformers for the specific task of Tm prediction, while acknowledging that larger models may offer broader multi-task capabilities.
Furthermore, the per-temperature-bin analysis reveals a critical limitation of current OGT predictors. PRIME and ThermoFormer achieve excellent performance in the 20–40°C range, where training data is abundant, but their error increases 4–5× in the 40–60°C range the textbook signature of mesophilic overfitting. This pattern is not a flaw of these models per se, but rather a reflection of the underlying data distribution: OGT databases are heavily biased toward mesophilic organisms, and models trained on such data inevitably learn to interpolate well in the dense region while failing to generalize to sparser thermophilic regimes. StableProt's error profile, while higher in absolute terms on mesophiles, is nearly uniform across all temperature bins. The model does not collapse on underrepresented thermophilic temperatures because the mesophilic subsampling strategy (ogt_subsample_meso_rate = 0.14) actively prevents overfitting to the dominant distribution. We argue that consistent, generalizable predictions across all temperature regimes are more valuable for enzyme engineering than overfitted mesophilic accuracy that fails on thermophilic targets. This is particularly important for bioprospecting applications where thermophilic enzymes are the primary targets.
Furthermore, our introduction of Confidence-Adjusted MAE (Int-MAE) addresses a major ethical and practical gap in computational biology. In wet-laboratory enzyme engineering, point predictions without confidence bounds are dangerous and economically wasteful. Knowing whether a prediction carries an uncertainty of ±1.5°C versus ±12.0°C dictates whether an experimentalist synthesizes a candidate gene. Because baseline predictors (TemBERTure, DeepSTABp, ESMStabP, PRIME) output deterministic point estimates without uncertainty intervals (σi = 0), they receive zero discount under Int-MAE. StableProt's heteroscedastic NLL head and 5-seed disagreement bounds provide statistically calibrated reliability intervals that mathematically penalize overconfidence while rewarding experimental precision.
Our bidirectional decontamination findings underscore the critical necessity of rigorous dataset auditing. The discovery that over 2,100 ProThermDB benchmark sequences overlapped with historical pre-training partitions in prior literature explains why legacy models reported inflated validation accuracies that failed to translate into laboratory success. By enforcing strict <30% sequence identity separation and pairing sequence language models with Foldseek 3Di structural alphabets (SaProt), StableProt successfully mitigates mesophilic probability collapse when evaluated on zero-shot out-of-distribution extremophiles.
Looking forward, StableProt provides an open-source, reproducible computational suite capable of guiding directed evolution, stabilizing industrial enzymes, and annotating metagenomic dark matter. Future extensions integrating dynamic molecular dynamics structural flexibility graphs could further refine predictions for cold-adapted psychrophilic variants.
