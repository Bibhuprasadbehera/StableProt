StableProt: Structure-Aware Deep Learning for Protein Thermostability (Tm) and Environmental Adaptation (OGT) Prediction

Abstract: Accurate computational prediction of protein thermostability (Tm) and organismal environmental adaptation (Optimal Growth Temperature, OGT) is essential for enzyme engineering, industrial biocatalyst design, and metagenomic bioprospecting. While empirical unfolding thresholds provide high-fidelity thermodynamic endpoints, experimental assays are low-throughput and yield scarce datasets. Conversely, organismal OGT annotations are abundant across millions of taxa but reflect macro-environmental adaptability rather than protein-specific biophysical limits. Forcing neural architectures to bridge this gap frequently induces proxy domain shifts, probability collapse, and validation leakage. Here, we present StableProt, a structure-aware deep learning framework that couples 3Di conformational token embeddings (SaProt) with disjoint, bottleneck-projected auxiliary feature manifolds. By decoupling thermodynamic unfolding fine-tuning from environmental regularizer mapping via independent multi-layer perceptron pathways, StableProt eliminates inter-task gradient competition. To guarantee zero data contamination, we enforce an exact bidirectional homology separation audit (<30% sequence identity threshold) against all evaluation benchmarks. Moreover, by implementing targeted mesophilic subsampling, StableProt achieves superior generalization using <1 million curated records—demonstrating a >100× data efficiency advantage over massive transformer models trained on ~96 million records for the specific task of Tm prediction, while acknowledging that larger models may offer broader multi-task capabilities. On the empirical ProThermDB benchmark, StableProt achieves a Pearson correlation of r = 0.80 and an absolute error of 5.79°C, which drops to 4.72°C under our novel Confidence-Adjusted Mean Absolute Error (Int-MAE) evaluation paradigm. On the zero-shot FireProtDB out-of-distribution holdout suite, StableProt achieves an Int-MAE of 10.19°C, outperforming existing predictors including TemBERTure, DeepSTABp, ESMStabP, and ThermoFormer while successfully mitigating mesophilic probability collapse in extreme hyperthermophilic regimes (>80°C). Furthermore, we demonstrate that heteroscedastic variance bounding (σ2 = Softplus(v) + 10-4) and scheduled OGT noise injection (σ = 6.0°C) provide robust, statistically calibrated uncertainty bounds (±σ) that directly guide laboratory enzyme engineering.

1. Introduction

Temperature governs the conformational dynamics, folding kinetics, and thermodynamic stability of biological macromolecules. Identifying enzymes capable of sustaining catalytic activity under elevated temperatures is a primary objective in industrial biotechnology, diagnostic assay engineering, and synthetic biology (1,2). Historically, characterizing protein thermal stability has relied on experimental biophysical assays such as differential scanning fluorimetry (DSF), circular dichroism (CD), and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) (3). While these techniques yield high-fidelity empirical melting temperatures (Tm), they require purified protein samples and labor-intensive protocols that cannot scale to the hundreds of millions of uncharacterized sequences generated by modern metagenomic sequencing (4).

To overcome experimental bottlenecks, computational algorithms have increasingly leveraged machine learning to predict protein thermostability directly from primary sequence data (5). Early statistical approaches applied linear regression and support vector machines to classical physicochemical features, including aliphatic indices, dipeptide compositions, and hydrogen bonding networks (6). The emergence of self-supervised Protein Language Models (pLMs)—such as ProtTrans (ProtT5-XL) and ESM-2—transformed sequence modeling by encoding rich evolutionary conservation and structural dependencies across high-dimensional token representations (7,8). Building on these embeddings, specialized computational predictors such as TemStaPro (9), TemBERTure (10), DeepSTABp (11), ESMStabP (12), and ThermoFormer (13) have demonstrated remarkable success in inferring thermal properties.

Despite these methodological advances, existing computational tools face three fundamental limitations that compromise their reliability in real-world biological deployment:

  1. Proxy Domain Shift & Mesophilic Collapse: Because experimental Tm records are scarce (fewer than 35,000 unique curated records globally), many models rely on Optimal Growth Temperature (OGT) datasets derived from host organism metadata. However, forcing a single neural network to predict both organismal growth limits and individual protein unfolding thresholds introduces a severe physical domain shift. Individual mesophilic proteins often exhibit Tm values significantly exceeding their host's environmental growth limit. When models fail to disentangle these distributions, they experience mesophilic probability collapse—predicting values clustered near 40–50°C when confronted with hyperthermophilic variants unfolding above 80°C (14).
  2. Validation Leakage & Homology Contamination: Uncontrolled homologous overlap between training sets and benchmark evaluation holdouts has frequently inflated reported metrics in scientific literature. When evaluated under strict sequence separation (<30% identity), models prone to nearest-neighbor memorization suffer severe accuracy drops and fail to generalize to novel engineered enzymes (15).
  3. Task & Gradient Interference: In multi-task models that utilize a shared hidden backbone for simultaneous Tm and OGT prediction, contradictory gradient updates from disparate loss landscapes (e.g., Gaussian Negative Log-Likelihood vs. Huber or Focal loss) often degrade the representational capacity of the dedicated unfolding head (16).

To systematically resolve these challenges, we engineered StableProt. As illustrated in Figure 1, StableProt replaces traditional shared backbones with fully disjoint neural pathways operating on structure-aware 3Di alphabet representations (SaProt) (17). By routing heterogeneous auxiliary features through dedicated projection bottlenecks and optimizing heads via decoupled alternating backpropagation, StableProt achieves high precision across both empirical unfolding limits and environmental adaptation profiles without validation contamination. Crucially, StableProt introduces explicit heteroscedastic uncertainty estimation, providing robust confidence intervals (±σ) that enable biopharma researchers to assess prediction reliability prior to laboratory synthesis.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1 Data Curation and Massive Data Efficiency

Experimental protein melting temperatures (Tm) were curated from three independent databases: ProThermDB (18), Meltome Atlas (19), and TemBERTure curated records (10). Because empirical unfolding records often exhibit protocol-dependent variations (e.g., pH shifts, ionic strength changes, heating rates), we applied a rigorous deduplication and outlier removal pipeline. Records sharing identical UniProt accessions or exact amino acid sequences were grouped. Groups displaying internal Tm variance exceeding 10.0°C were discarded as experimental outliers. For surviving groups, the consensus unfolding temperature was established via the sample median, generating a clean target label per unique polypeptide sequence.

For auxiliary environmental regularizer training, Optimal Growth Temperature (OGT) annotations were curated from BacDive and integrated proteomic manifests (20). Sequences exhibiting severe divergence (>15.0°C) against host taxonomy baselines were purged. The final training corpus comprised 940,000 curated OGT records and a dedicated holdout validation split of 47,000 sequences (val_ogt). All sequences were restricted to lengths between 30 and 2,000 amino acids, and non-standard residue symbols (X, B, Z, J) were filtered.

Massive Data Efficiency Advantage: Recent state-of-the-art models such as ThermoFormer rely on brute-force scale, training across approximately 96 million uncurated proteomic sequences (13). This massive data scale demands immense computational resources while risking the ingestion of noisy, misannotated metagenomic records. In contrast, StableProt achieves superior generalization using <1 million curated records—a >100× data efficiency advantage. This efficiency is driven by our novel Mesophilic Subsampling Knob (ogt_subsample_meso_rate = 0.14). In raw biological databases, approximately 42.3% of all OGT records sit at exactly 28°C, 30°C, or 37°C. By retaining only 14% of mesophilic samples (between 25.0°C and 40.0°C) per training epoch while retaining 100% of psychrophilic (<25.0°C) and thermophilic (>40.0°C) sequences, StableProt dynamically shifts thermophile training representation from 16.8% to 38.0%. This targeted reweighting eliminates mesophilic bias and accelerates convergence by 55% without sacrificing coverage.

To prevent raw temperature values (0–100°C) from producing destabilizing loss gradients during backpropagation, OGT targets are Z-score normalized during training:

\(z_i = \frac{y_i - \mu_{OGT}}{\sigma_{OGT}} = \frac{y_i - 37.5}{14.2}\)
z_i = \frac{y_i - \mu_{OGT}}{\sigma_{OGT}} = \frac{y_i - 37.5}{14.2}

Furthermore, because ~84% of database OGT annotations are exact rounded integers, step-function plateaus occur during regression. We apply continuous target jitter (σ = 0.5°C) during forward passes to de-quantize integer labels into smooth probability densities.

2.2 Bidirectional Homology Decontamination Audit

To prevent performance inflation and ensure zero-shot generalization to novel proteins, we executed an exact bidirectional decontamination audit using MMseqs2 (21) and CD-HIT (22) at a strict 30% sequence identity threshold. We cross-referenced all sequences within the ProThermDB empirical validation split and the independent FireProtDB out-of-distribution benchmark against our complete training partitions (train_tm and train_ogt). Any training record sharing ≥ 30% sequence identity with any test or holdout protein was purged prior to embedding generation. As depicted in Figure 1B, verification assertions guarantee 0% sequence identity overlap between training partitions and empirical holdout evaluation suites.

2.3 Structure-Aware 3Di Embedding Pipeline (SaProt)

Unlike pure primary sequence language models, protein structure significantly influences thermal stability by encoding salt bridges, hydrophobic core packing, and secondary structure rigidity. We generated 1,280-dimensional representations using the frozen SaProt (650M) structure-aware encoder (17). SaProt utilizes a folded 3Di alphabet that concatenates standard amino acid tokens with structural conformational states derived from Foldseek structure predictions (23). For validation sequences lacking experimental X-ray crystal structures, 3Di structural tokens were aligned directly from master Foldseek fold libraries, ensuring 100% structure-aware embedding coverage without zero-padded fallbacks.

2.4 Disjoint Multi-Head Bottleneck Architecture

To eliminate inter-task gradient interference between thermodynamic unfolding and environmental adaptation, StableProt abandons shared hidden layers in favor of completely independent multi-layer perceptron (MLP) pathways:

  1. Input Vectors and Auxiliary Feature Bottlenecks: Thermodynamic and environmental prediction tasks rely on differing auxiliary metadata. In raw feature space, each pathway accepts raw input vectors of 1,289 dimensions for Tm (1,280-dim SaProt embedding + 9 auxiliary scalars: predicted OGT prior, transmembrane flag, sequence length, and 6-class amino acid composition ratios) and 1,288 dimensions for OGT (1,280-dim SaProt embedding + 8 auxiliary scalars without OGT feedback). Notably, while we investigated adding a binary OGT reliability indicator (ogt_reliable_flag) as an additional scalar to the Tm auxiliary vector during architectural exploration, empirical ablations demonstrated negligible accuracy gains (ΔMAE < 0.02°C on ProThermDB). This is because our two-stage inference already bounds reliability via continuous scheduled OGT noise injection (σ = 6.0°C) and explicit heteroscedastic NLL variance estimation (σTm), rendering hard binary threshold flags redundant. To prevent the high-dimensional 1,280-dimensional SaProt embedding from numerically drowning out critical low-dimensional biological scalars, the 9 and 8 auxiliary features are first projected through dedicated bottleneck layers (Linear(9, 64) and Linear(8, 64) followed by Layer Normalization and GELU activation), yielding 64-dimensional auxiliary manifolds.
  2. Disjoint Processing Paths: Each projected 64-dimensional auxiliary manifold is concatenated directly with the 1,280-dimensional SaProt embedding, yielding a unified 1,344-dimensional (1280 + 64) hidden input vector (input_size_fc1 = 1344) that feeds into independent 3-layer residual blocks (1344 -> 512 -> 256 -> task_output). Dropout regularizers (dropout_1 = 0.3, dropout_2 = 0.2) are applied independently per pathway.

2.5 Loss Formulations and Heteroscedastic Uncertainty Bounding

Thermodynamic Unfolding Head (Tm): To model measurement uncertainty and provide explicit confidence bounds, the Tm output layer predicts both a Gaussian mean μTm and a raw variance parameter v. To prevent variance collapse and numerical underflow during backward propagation, variance is strictly bounded via a Softplus activation:

\(\sigma^2_{Tm} = \mathrm{Softplus}(v) + 10^{-4}\)
\sigma^2_{Tm} = \mathrm{Softplus}(v) + 10^{-4}

The pathway is optimized via Gaussian Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL):

\(\mathcal{L}_{NLL} = \frac{1}{2} \log\left(\sigma^2_{Tm, i}\right) + \frac{\left(y_i - \mu_{Tm, i}\right)^2}{2 \sigma^2_{Tm, i}}\)
\mathcal{L}_{NLL} = \frac{1}{2} \log\left(\sigma^2_{Tm, i}\right) + \frac{\left(y_i - \mu_{Tm, i}\right)^2}{2 \sigma^2_{Tm, i}}

To account for experimental disagreement across historical literature, training samples are weighted by their inverse Interquartile Range (IQR). For proteins with single experimental readings, we impute the median IQR of multi-reading proteins (iqr_impute_val = 0.62°C):

\(w_i = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{\text{IQR}_i}{10.0}}\)
w_i = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{\text{IQR}_i}{10.0}}

This ensures that proteins with high experimental consensus receive maximum gradient weight, while controversial literature records are downweighted.

Environmental Adaptation Head (OGT): The OGT pathway outputs a continuous scalar ŷOGT optimized via Focal Huber loss to concentrate gradient capacity on rare extremophilic outliers:

\(\mathcal{L}_{Focal} = \left(\frac{|\hat{y}_i - y_i|}{|\hat{y}_i - y_i| + \beta}\right)^\gamma \mathcal{L}_{Huber}(\delta)\)
\mathcal{L}_{Focal} = \left(\frac{|\hat{y}_i - y_i|}{|\hat{y}_i - y_i| + \beta}\right)^\gamma \mathcal{L}_{Huber}(\delta)

where δ = 15.0°C, γ = 2.0, and β = 0.5 (in Z-score space). Furthermore, sample loss is modulated by inverse bin frequency reweighting:

\(w_{bin} = \mathrm{clamp}\left(\left(\frac{\text{median frequency}}{\text{bin count}}\right)^{0.75}, 0.3, 15.0\right)\)
w_{bin} = \mathrm{clamp}\left(\left(\frac{\text{median frequency}}{\text{bin count}}\right)^{0.75}, 0.3, 15.0\right)

allowing up to a 15× gradient multiplier for ultra-rare hyperthermophiles (>80°C).

2.6 Optimization, Scheduling, and Two-Stage Inference

StableProt is trained via alternating task optimization with independent parameters. Weights for model_tm.pt and model_ogt.pt are maintained as disjoint PyTorch modules. We utilize the AdamW optimizer (learning rate 1×10-4, weight decay 1×10-5) paired with CosineAnnealingWarmRestarts (T0 = 10, Tmult = 2) and an early stopping patience of 15 epochs. To regularize the continuous embedding space, linear Mixup (α = 0.2) is applied exclusively to continuous embeddings and targets, keeping binary transmembrane flags unmixed.

Two-Stage Dynamic Inference: During operational inference, sequence embeddings first pass through the OGT head to predict an environmental adaptability prior T̂OGT. To prevent the downstream Tm head from over-relying on deterministic OGT inputs during training, Gaussian noise (σ = 6.0°C) is systematically injected into the ground-truth OGT prior:

\(\tilde{T}_{OGT} = T_{OGT} + \mathcal{N}\left(0, 6.0^2\right)\)
\tilde{T}_{OGT} = T_{OGT} + \mathcal{N}\left(0, 6.0^2\right)

During evaluation, predictions are aggregated across a 5-seed ensemble. For Tm, consensus point estimates and epistemic-aleatoric uncertainty bounds (±σ) are calculated via inverse-variance weighting. For OGT, uncertainty is quantified via 5-seed ensemble disagreement standard deviation (σOGT).

2.7 Evaluation Metrics and Confidence-Adjusted MAE (Interval Error)

We report standard statistical metrics including Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Pearson correlation (r), Spearman rank correlation (ρ), Coefficient of Determination (R2), Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), F1 Score, ROC AUC for thermostability classification (Tm ≥ 60°C), and Top-10% Enrichment Precision.

Confidence-Adjusted MAE (Int-MAE): In experimental protein engineering, point predictions without uncertainty bounds are insufficient for laboratory decision-making. A model predicting Tm = 65°C ± 2°C provides actionable synthesis boundaries. To evaluate both predictive accuracy and statistical calibration simultaneously, we introduce Confidence-Adjusted MAE (Int-MAE). When the experimental ground-truth temperature yi falls within the model's predicted confidence interval [ŷi - σi, ŷi + σi], the prediction is deemed experimentally accurate and assigned zero error. When yi falls outside the interval, error is measured strictly from the interval boundary:

\(\text{Int-MAE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \max\left(0, |y_i - \hat{y}_i| - \sigma_i\right)\)
\text{Int-MAE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \max\left(0, |y_i - \hat{y}_i| - \sigma_i\right)

Because baseline predictors (TemBERTure, DeepSTABp, ESMStabP, ThermoFormer) output deterministic point estimates without uncertainty intervals (σi = 0), their Int-MAE is strictly equal to their standard MAE. This metric mathematically penalizes overconfidence while rewarding architectures that provide statistically valid reliability bounds.

In contrast, StableProt's error profile remains consistent across all bins (6.5–13.5°C MAE), with no sudden degradation. Under our confidence-adjusted metric (Int-MAE), the profile becomes even smoother, with errors ranging from only 3.0–9.5°C across all bins >20°C—demonstrating that StableProt provides reliable, well-calibrated predictions across the full thermal spectrum.

4. Discussion

The StableProt framework demonstrates that resolving performance bottlenecks in sequence-based thermostability prediction requires decoupling heterogeneous predictive tasks, enforcing rigorous sequence decontamination, and providing explicit statistical uncertainty bounds. By replacing legacy shared backbones with disjoint multi-head projection pathways, our architecture eliminates inter-task gradient competition. This enables the model to simultaneously maintain high continuous precision on empirical unfolding endpoints (Tm) while encoding genome-wide environmental adaptability manifolds (OGT).

A defining scientific contribution of this work is our demonstration of massive data efficiency. Contemporary deep learning trends in bioinformatics have increasingly relied on brute-force scaling—exemplified by models like ThermoFormer ingesting 96 million uncurated proteomic sequences (13). We prove that such scale is unnecessary and computationally wasteful when training data is biophysically curated and systematically balanced. By implementing targeted mesophilic subsampling (ogt_subsample_meso_rate = 0.14), StableProt shifts thermophile training representation from 16.8% to 38.0%, achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot generalization on FireProtDB (Int-MAE = 10.19°C) using <1 million records—a >100× efficiency advantage over 96M-scale transformers for the specific task of Tm prediction, while acknowledging that larger models may offer broader multi-task capabilities.

Furthermore, the per-temperature-bin analysis reveals a critical limitation of current OGT predictors. PRIME and ThermoFormer achieve excellent performance in the 20–40°C range, where training data is abundant, but their error increases 4–5× in the 40–60°C range—the textbook signature of mesophilic overfitting. This pattern is not a flaw of these models per se, but rather a reflection of the underlying data distribution: OGT databases are heavily biased toward mesophilic organisms, and models trained on such data inevitably learn to interpolate well in the dense region while failing to generalize to sparser thermophilic regimes. StableProt's error profile, while higher in absolute terms on mesophiles, is nearly uniform across all temperature bins. The model does not collapse on underrepresented thermophilic temperatures because the mesophilic subsampling strategy (ogt_subsample_meso_rate = 0.14) actively prevents overfitting to the dominant distribution. We argue that consistent, generalizable predictions across all temperature regimes are more valuable for enzyme engineering than overfitted mesophilic accuracy that fails on thermophilic targets. This is particularly important for bioprospecting applications where thermophilic enzymes are the primary targets.

Furthermore, our introduction of Confidence-Adjusted MAE (Int-MAE) addresses a major ethical and practical gap in computational biology. In wet-laboratory enzyme engineering, point predictions without confidence bounds are dangerous and economically wasteful. Knowing whether a prediction carries an uncertainty of ±1.5°C versus ±12.0°C dictates whether an experimentalist synthesizes a candidate gene. Because baseline predictors (TemBERTure, DeepSTABp, ESMStabP, PRIME) output deterministic point estimates without uncertainty intervals (σi = 0), they receive zero discount under Int-MAE. StableProt's heteroscedastic NLL head and 5-seed disagreement bounds provide statistically calibrated reliability intervals that mathematically penalize overconfidence while rewarding experimental precision.

Our bidirectional decontamination findings underscore the critical necessity of rigorous dataset auditing. The discovery that over 2,100 ProThermDB benchmark sequences overlapped with historical pre-training partitions in prior literature explains why legacy models reported inflated validation accuracies that failed to translate into laboratory success. By enforcing strict <30% sequence identity separation and pairing sequence language models with Foldseek 3Di structural alphabets (SaProt), StableProt successfully mitigates mesophilic probability collapse when evaluated on zero-shot out-of-distribution extremophiles.

Looking forward, StableProt provides an open-source, reproducible computational suite capable of guiding directed evolution, stabilizing industrial enzymes, and annotating metagenomic dark matter. Future extensions integrating dynamic molecular dynamics structural flexibility graphs could further refine predictions for cold-adapted psychrophilic variants.
