Supplementary Note 1. Gradient decoupling

The motivating measurement is the shared-backbone control. When a single hidden stack is optimized for Tm under Gaussian negative log-likelihood and for OGT under focal Huber loss, the two objectives pull the shared parameters in partially opposing directions. Writing the two task gradients with respect to the shared parameters \(\theta\):

\[ \cos\theta_{\text{grad}} \;=\; \frac{\big\langle \nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{T_m},\ \nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{OGT} \big\rangle}{\big\Vert \nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{T_m} \big\Vert \, \big\Vert \nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{OGT} \big\Vert} \;=\; -0.045 \quad \text{(shared backbone, mean over 150 sampled steps)} \](S1)
\cos\theta_{\text{grad}} \;=\; \frac{\big\langle \nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{T_m},\ \nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{OGT} \big\rangle}{\big\Vert \nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{T_m} \big\Vert \, \big\Vert \nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{OGT} \big\Vert} \;=\; -0.045 \quad \text{(shared backbone, mean over 150 sampled steps)}

The resulting model is 1.50 °C worse on ProThermDB than the disjoint design (7.61 against 6.11 °C at the matched development stage, Table S2). That penalty is the experimental result.

We state explicitly what the disjoint architecture does not demonstrate. Because the two pathways share no trainable parameters, the parameter sets are disjoint, \(\theta_{T_m} \cap \theta_{OGT} = \varnothing\), so each task gradient is identically zero on the other task's coordinates and the cosine similarity is zero as a matter of definition. Reporting \(\cos\theta = 0\) for StableProt is therefore not evidence of anything; it follows from the construction. The cosine analysis belongs in the paper as an explanation of why the shared model underperforms, and not as a demonstration that the disjoint model works.

The design is best described as a shared frozen representation with task-specific pathways and a directed OGT-to-Tm link. This resolves the apparent tension between two claims made in the main text, that disjoint pathways beat a shared backbone and that OGT information helps Tm, because they concern different things. Gradient interference is a statement about sharing parameters. The prior is a statement about sharing information, one-way, at the level of predictions. Both can hold simultaneously, and the measurements say they do: the shared backbone costs 1.50 °C, while the prior sub-ladder in Table S2b shows the informative prior is worth 2.66 °C of validation error against the oracle and 1.27 °C against a constant. The term "multi-task learning" is avoided throughout, since the two objectives are never optimized jointly through shared weights.

Supplementary Note 2. Negative results

Five interventions were tested and rejected. They are recorded here because each is a natural next step that a reader may otherwise assume was overlooked.

A constant OGT prior. Replacing the predicted prior with a constant improves ProThermDB error from 6.16 to 5.71 °C. It was rejected on architectural grounds: the two pathways share no trainable parameters, so a constant in the prior column is absorbed into the following layer's bias and carries exactly zero information, which reduces the model to two independent networks and removes the coupling the design exists to exploit.

Retraining the Tm head on the predicted prior. A variant trained directly on predicted rather than true OGT reached 3.98 °C validation error but only 7.40 °C on ProThermDB, against 6.16 for the published model. Training on the predicted prior allowed the head to exploit prior structure specific to the training distribution, and the removal of the prior-jitter regularizer amplified the effect. On this task, validation error does not rank configurations, and no Tm configuration should be selected on validation alone.

Uncertainty-gated coupling. Supplying the Tm head with the OGT head's own uncertainty alongside its point prediction, so that the head could discount an unreliable prior, produced results indistinguishable from the ungated variant on validation and on ProThermDB (3.98 against 3.99, and 7.40 against 7.42), separating only on FireProtDB (12.35 against 12.75). One benchmark out of three is not support, and the mechanism is not claimed.

Supervising σ with measured inter-laboratory spread. The interquartile range across repeated literature measurements has a median of 0.00 °C and a mean of 0.72 °C, against model errors averaging 6.16 °C. Label noise is negligible next to model error, so this target would drive severe under-dispersion and destroy the calibration that already works.

Post-hoc recalibration of the predictions. Cross-fitted isotonic regression on ProThermDB appears to reduce error to 4.76 °C, beating every baseline. It is circular. Fitted instead on the validation split the model's slope is 0.983 rather than 0.646, and applying that map blind to ProThermDB changes the error only from 6.178 to 6.104 °C. The apparent gain was the recalibration fitting the test set.

One intervention did work and is reported in the main text: training the variance component against a detached mean fixed the scale of the interval, taking the required calibration factor from 5.5 to below 1.2 on OGT. It did not make the variance informative per protein. Scale and informativeness are separable properties of a heteroscedastic head, and this work separates them cleanly.
